01 / Overview

Executive summary

Core thesis

The group's global momentum was not destroyed by one bad concept. It was destroyed by a leadership team that let artists absorb a mistake the agency itself approved, scheduled, and produced.

Group background

Debuted under S2 Entertainment in July 2023 with strong vocal execution and mature R and B or hip hop production, positioning the act as one of the more Western leaning rookie groups of its generation.

Competitive advantage

Members Julie and Belle grew up in the United States, which gave the group an unusually high baseline of Western cultural fluency compared to most rookie acts.

Flashpoint event

An agency sanctioned birthday livestream in April 2025 undid a large share of the international trust the group had built over its first two years.

02 / Chronology

How it unfolded

Pre debut

An old clip resurfaces

Footage from Julie's pre debut training years surfaced showing her reciting a racial slur while covering a rap song.

Response: a direct, personal apology, accepted by international fans as an isolated trainee era mistake.
Weeks before

A quiet warning sign

Member Belle messaged fans on an artist platform beforehand, asking them to promise not to leave the fandom, a sign someone inside the group sensed risk without anyone stopping the plan.

April 2, 2025

The birthday livestream airs

An agency organized broadcast for Julie's birthday, themed around old school hip hop, featured cornrows, gold chains, exaggerated accents, and caricatured humor.

Immediate backlash across X, TikTok, and Reddit within hours.
April 3, 2025

A statement that made things worse

S2 Entertainment framed the broadcast as a tribute to hip hop culture, a response many read as evasive rather than accountable.

Western music media rejected the statement as performative.
April 5, 2025

Four handwritten letters

Management directed all four members to post separate handwritten apology notes, then casual social posts followed soon after, signaling a mismatched, poorly coordinated internal response.

Over 200,000 Instagram unfollows recorded within days.
Following months

Boycotts settle in

Key international fan communities and Black and Latino content creators organized active boycotts while Korean and Japanese streaming metrics stayed largely undisturbed.

03 / Divergence

One incident, two markets

Global / Western fallout

Sharp and lasting

  • 200,000+ Instagram unfollows recorded within days of the broadcast. A visible, countable signal of trust lost in real time.
  • Active boycotts organized by international fan bases and Black and Latino creators. Backlash extended past casual fans into organized communities.
  • Western music outlets rejected the agency's statement as evasive. Media coverage amplified the crisis rather than absorbing it.
Domestic / East Asian reaction

Quiet and unaffected

  • Korean and Japanese streaming metrics saw minimal chart disruption. Commercial performance at home barely registered the story.
  • Mainstream domestic outlets largely ignored the controversy. Little local media pressure meant little internal urgency.
  • Regional audiences kept prioritizing new music over global discourse. A structural gap in how each market weighs cultural missteps.
04 / Root cause

The deflection loop

Step 1

Concept clears internal review

Costumes, props, and schedule pass through agency pipelines with no real brand safety check.

→
Step 2

No one stops the broadcast

Staff on site let the livestream run despite clear, visible cultural missteps during filming.

→
Step 3

Agency issues evasive language

Leadership claims it did not realize the content would reinforce stereotypes, avoiding direct ownership.

→
Step 4

Artists absorb the backlash

Members are pushed to write personal apology letters for a decision the company made and approved.

Every future concept risk still runs through the same unaudited pipeline until Step 1 is fixed.
05 / Strategy

What agencies should take from this

01

Make cultural review part of the pipeline

Global acts need mandatory cross cultural checks before any concept is filmed, not after it airs.

02

Leadership owns approved concepts

If management signs off on a concept, management answers for it publicly, not the artists who performed it.

03

Respect the genre, not the costume

Borrowing from a musical culture means understanding its history first, rather than treating it as a styling gimmick.
